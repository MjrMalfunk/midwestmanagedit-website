Support model

Remote-first, local when it matters.

Most support can happen faster remotely: troubleshooting, user help, Microsoft 365 administration, patching review, security checks, and routine follow-up. When hands-on work is genuinely needed, local onsite help can still be part of the plan.

The goal is simple: resolve what can be handled quickly, coordinate what needs hands-on attention, and keep support from turning into emergency-only chaos.
Relationship lanes

Three lanes that frame the relationship.

Manage IT gives the business a solid operating baseline. Protect IT is the strongest lead for risk-aware teams that want backup and better protection built in. Govern IT expands administration and oversight for businesses that need more depth and coordination.

  • Manage IT for foundational support and steadier operations.
  • Protect IT for stronger security posture and backup coverage.
  • Govern IT for broader administrative lift and governance.

Common questions
Do we have to understand the plan and the service lane first?

No. Start with the friction point. The site is structured to make the categories clearer, not to demand a perfect self-diagnosis before a first conversation.

Can support start with one pain point and grow later?

Yes. Many relationships begin around the most pressing friction point, then expand into a broader support model as the environment matures.

Will local onsite help still be possible?

Yes. The support model is remote-first, but nearby onsite work can still be part of the relationship when it adds real value.
